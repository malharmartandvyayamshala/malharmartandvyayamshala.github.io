# Shri Malhar Martand Vyayamshala, Indore — History & Achievements from the Press Archive

**श्री मल्हार मार्तण्ड व्यायामशाला (मल्हारी मार्तण्ड / हरचंद उस्ताद व्यायामशाला), इन्दौर**

This document records everything printed about the akhada in the family's collection of newspaper and magazine cuttings, plus one original award certificate. It covers **25 items published between 1979 and 2008**, describing a history that begins around **1861**.

All cuttings are in Hindi. Headlines are quoted in the original Hindi with an English translation. Names are spelled as printed; "P." in the originals stands for *Pahalwan* (wrestler).

## 3. The akhada's history as told by the press

### 3.1 Founding and the lineage of Ustads

The akhada's early story is told in five articles: 1985, 1986, 1993, and two from 2000. They agree on the broad outline and differ on details (see [Section 6](#6-where-the-sources-disagree)).

- **Moroba Ustad** came from **Rampura-Bhanpura**, then part of the Holkar State and known for the cannons made there. He was an expert in strength and stamina training, "a true *kalavant*". The 1985 and 1993 articles name him as the founder.
- His son **Rambhau More Ustad** was called to Indore by **Maharaja Tukojirao Holkar II** to train the Holkar army. He also received military training himself. According to the 1986 article, he founded the **Shri Malhar Martand Akhada in 1861** "to draw ordinary citizens, too, towards wrestling", on the occasion of **Ahilya Janmotsav**. He later set up an akhada at the **Dhar fort**, where he trained three disciples: **Harchand, Bahadar Khan and Shankarrao Sawant**.
- **Harchand Ganpat Mali (Ustad 1909–1952)** came to Indore from Dhar during the famine of the early 1900s. With the help of his guru's widow **Laxmibai Maa Saheb**, he established the vyayamshala in Indore in Rambhau's memory and named it **"Malhari Martand"**. In **1909** he received a **sanad from the Holkar Household Department**. Over the following decades the akhada came to be known by his name as well: *Harchand Ustad Vyayamshala*.
- **Shankarlal Mali**, Harchand's son, was tied the **Ustad's pagdi in 1952**. He was still leading the akhada when it received the 2005 award and the modern-hall bhoomi-pujan, and again in 2008.

### 3.2 The Holkar State's akhada

The 1985 feature by **Dr. Sudhakar Amritphale** says:

> "It was called the Holkar State's akhada… Being a government akhada, it received the first salute in the processions of Ganeshotsav, Ahilya Utsav, Dol Gyaras and Muharram… This was the only government akhada of Holkar State, with about 50–60 branches spread to villages of the State. The State awarded prizes to the akhada's renowned wrestlers."

The same article notes that **the record of these prizes is preserved in the Holkar State records**.

**Training:** Holkar army soldiers trained here in **lathi, pata, banethi, spear, lance and horse-riding**. Citizens trained in **wrestling, gadka-fari, udi, rope-climbing, mallakhamb and gymnastics** (#14, #18, #20, #21).

**The only akhada that produced *kalavants*:** *Khel Halchal* (2000) wrote that "it was not that this akhada failed to produce great wrestlers, but during the Holkar State it was **the only vyayamshala that produced *kalavants***" (artist-performers of the martial arts).

**State festivals:**
- Devi Ahilya's palki procession
- Dussehra
- Muharram (including the government tazia from the Imambada)
- Ganesh Chaturthi
- Dol Gyaras
- Champa Shashthi
- Devshayani Ekadashi
- the Maharaja's birthday and coronation day

### 3.3 The freedom movement

- The **1985** feature (#20) and its **2000** restatement (#17) say the government akhada and its branches "**carried the national movement to remote villages and towns**". They built the health of young people and helped win central India's support for the freedom struggle.
- **Nav Bharat (2000)** (#16) records that **the British wanted to ban the akhada, but Harchand Ustad took it out in procession anyway**, out of devotion to his faith. The cutting does not give a year; it was during Harchand's tenure (1909–1947).

### 3.6 The branch akhadas

The 2000 *Khel Halchal* feature (#14) lists vyayamshalas founded by ustads trained at Malhar Martand. The 1993 article (#13) gives a similar list.

- **Balbhim Vyayamshala, Banganga** (Gajadhar Ustad). It produced **Rustam-e-M.P. Amarsingh**, Ramavtar, Nibbu, **Radheshyam Arya**, **Ramlal Yadav** (later an MLA) and **Bante**.
- **Nanku Ustad's vyayamshala, Banganga.** Nanku Ustad was a weapons-art expert. **Narendra Borasi**, a university gold medallist, trained here.
- **Other branches:**

### #20 — Dainik Swadesh, 15 August 1985

**"मालवा के अखाड़ों की राष्ट्रीय आंदोलन में भूमिका"**
*The role of Malwa's akhadas in the national movement.* By **Dr. Sudhakar Amritphale**.

- **Context:** the princely states sided with the British, so freedom fighters used akhadas and clubs to organise. Indore's akhadas at Chhatripura, Malipura and Krishnapura, and the Ganesh Mandal Vyayamshala, were important centres. Indore's akhada culture began when the Punjabi wrestler **Boota** came to Indore under Tukojirao II.
- **On Malhar Martand (highlighted in yellow):**
  - "After 1857, in the reign of Maharaja Tukojirao (II)… an akhada named **'Shri Malhar Martand Vyayamshala'** was founded **by Shri Moroba Ustad**."
  - It began in **Boliya's Payga**, moved to **Rambagh** ten years later, and in 1985 stood on the **Krishnapura riverbank**.
  - After Moroba, **Shri Rambhau More** took charge.
  - It was the **Holkar State's akhada**. Soldiers learned **lathi and horse-riding** there, along with Indian sports.
  - It received the **first salute** in the Ganeshotsav, Ahilya Utsav, Dol Gyaras and Muharram processions.
  - "**In 1909 the akhada's management came into the hands of Shri Harchand Ganpat Mali.**"
  - It was "**the only government akhada of Holkar State**", with **50–60 branches**.
  - "The State awarded prizes to the akhada's renowned wrestlers."

### #21 — Newspaper not identified, 1986

**"इन्दौर का मल्हार मार्तण्ड अखाड़ा"** — kicker: **"१२५ वर्ष पुराना"** (125 years old)
*Indore's Malhar Martand Akhada.* By **Nagendra Azad**.

- **Holkar patronage:** Maharaja Tukojirao Holkar II, "maker of modern Indore", reigned for 42 years and encouraged wrestling to keep his army fit. He summoned **Rambhau More Ustad** to train soldiers in **lathi, pata, banethi, spear and lance**.
- **Founding:** "**In 1861 Rambhau established the Shri Malhar Martand Akhada on Hamilton Road**", to bring wrestling to citizens too. It was founded on **Ahilya Janmotsav**.
- **State role:** it performed before the Darbar at all state ceremonies, including the **Maharaja's birthday and coronation day**. A **branch was opened at Dhar** on the Maharaja's order.
- **Key dates given:**
  - **1909:** Harchand Ustad receives the akhada from the Household Department; ₹16 grant
  - **1927:** registered
  - **1952:** **Shankar Pahalwan tied the Ustad's pagdi**
- **Wrestlers:**
  - **Beni Pahalwan** (45), the akhada's mainstay, had trained new wrestlers for 12 years.
  - **Omprakash Pahalwan**, of this akhada, held the **Rustam-e-Indore** and **Indore Shri** titles.
  - Earlier names listed include Ramchandra, Vitthal, Ramlal, Gopal, Deva, Babu Banke, Ramanand, Mannu Kala, Amarsingh, Ramautar, Radheshyam, Nibbu and Meghraj.
- **Problems:** the MP Social Welfare grant (₹800–1,000) had not been received for several years, and wrestlers trained beside the polluted Krishnapura river.
- **Photo:** "Omprakash, **Indore Shri**, with the **Rustam-e-Indore shield and gurj**. Beside him: **Ustad Shankar Pahalwan** and **trainer Beni Pahalwan**."

## 6. Where the sources disagree

The cuttings were written over three decades by different authors, often from oral tradition. Where they differ, both versions are recorded above. The main differences are:

1. **Who founded the akhada.**
   - Moroba Ustad (1985, 1993)
   - Rambhau More (1986, 2000 *Khel Halchal*)
   - Harchand as Rambhau More's disciple (2000 *Kranti Kathan*)

   Read together, these describe one lineage: **Moroba → Rambhau More → Harchand**.

2. **Which Maharaja was on the throne.** Most sources say Tukojirao Holkar II; the 1993 article says Shivajirao Holkar.
   - Tukojirao II reigned 1844–1886, which fits 1861.
   - Shivajirao reigned 1886–1903, which fits the famine years, when Harchand moved to Indore.

3. **"128 years old" (2004).** This headline does not match 1861. The 1986 article called the akhada "125 years old", which does fit 1861. The 2004 text closely follows the 1986 article, so the "128" appears to be a carry-over or misprint.

4. **Early locations.**
   - 1985: Boliya's Payga → Rambagh → Krishnapura riverbank
   - 2000: Boliya's Payga in 1909 → the present Juna Tukoganj site
   - 1986: founded on Hamilton Road
   - The 2000 *Kranti Kathan* article says the present site "near Paliwal Dharmshala, Juna Tukoganj" is on Hamilton Road.

5. **Year of the move to the present site.** 1944 (*Khel Halchal*, 2000) or 1948 (*Kranti Kathan*, 2000); both name Maharaja Yeshwantrao Holkar.

6. **First committee.** 1920 under Balarao Deshpande (2000), or formed at registration in 1927 under Bala Saheb Deshpande (1993).

7. **Shared text.** The 2000 *Dainik Bhaskar* heritage article (#17) closely follows the 1985 *Swadesh* article (#20), so the "first salute" and "50–60 branches" claims trace back to one original source.

8. **Dates and names that differ from the family's notes:**
   - **#23:** the family note says 26 Jan 2004, but the certificate is printed 26 Jan 2005.
   - **#19:** recorded as *Dainik Jagran*, but the printed label on the cutting reads "दैनिक भास्कर इन्दौर".
   - **#7:** recorded as 21 Jan 2004, but the dateline reads 23 January and the handwriting "24/1/04".
   - **#24:** the day and month of the handwritten date are not certain.
